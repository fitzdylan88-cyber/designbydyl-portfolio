## The problem

Support agents spent the first minutes of every ticket working out what it
was actually about. Every new agent learned the triage rules from whoever sat
next to them, so the rules drifted.

<Callout label="Insight">
The slow part wasn't answering. It was deciding who should answer.
</Callout>

## How I used AI

I wrote the triage rules as a rubric, then gave the model graded examples
so that its reasoning, not just its label, could be reviewed.

<PromptBlock tool="Claude" prompt={`You are triaging support tickets.
Score each against the rubric below, then explain the score in one line.
<rubric>…</rubric>
<examples>…three graded examples…</examples>`}>
Category: Billing · Confidence: high · Reason: mentions a duplicate charge and includes a transaction date.
</PromptBlock>

## What I'd do differently

- Build the eval set on day one, not in week four.
- Put agents in the rubric-writing room earlier.
