# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

- **Design leads and hiring managers**: heads of design and design directors deciding whether Dylan is a senior or lead-level product designer. They read case studies closely and judge thinking as much as pixels: problem framing, decisions, tradeoffs, outcomes.
- **Recruiters**: skim in seconds for role, seniority, location and proof. They need the headline facts immediately, without hunting.
- **Founders and freelance clients**: evaluating Dylan for a project. They want evidence that things actually ship and work.

Context: usually a laptop, opened from a LinkedIn message, a CV link or an application. It's one of many portfolios they'll open that week, so it has seconds to earn attention and minutes to earn a conversation.

## Product Purpose

A job-search portfolio for Dylan Fitzpatrick, a Dublin-based product designer with 8+ years of experience who now designs *and* builds with AI (Claude, Claude Code). It shows case studies from employer work, shipped side projects, and experiments.

The site is itself evidence: a product designer's portfolio built to a high craft bar shows the claim ("I design and build") rather than stating it.

Success: the visitor asks for a conversation. Proxies are time on case studies, clicks through to the next piece, and contact clicks.

## Brand Personality

**Calm, sharp, ambitious.**

- Calm: understated surfaces, generous space, nothing shouting for attention. Confidence doesn't need volume.
- Sharp: precise typography, a real grid, exact motion, specific writing. Every detail looks decided.
- Ambitious: signature moments (the hero field, the cover morph, the cursor) show range and appetite, without turning into a showreel.

Voice: first person, plain, specific. Leads with problems and decisions, not adjectives. Never salesy.

## Anti-references

- **Generic AI/SaaS template**: gradient blobs, glass cards, shadcn defaults, "Hi, I'm X 👋", hero-metric strips.
- **Dribbble-shot showcase**: glossy mockups with no thinking, process or outcomes behind them.
- **Over-animated Awwwards site**: motion for its own sake, scroll-jacking, loaders, anything that slows reading or makes content wait for choreography.
- **Corporate CV page**: safe, list-heavy, personality-free.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't claim.** The site's own craft is the first case study. If a claim can be demonstrated (build quality, motion, AI workflow), demonstrate it.
2. **Thinking over decoration.** Case studies foreground problems, decisions and tradeoffs. Visuals serve the argument.
3. **Motion earns its place.** Each motion has one job (orientation, continuity, feedback) and never blocks content. Calm by default, expressive at a few chosen moments.
4. **Ten seconds, then ten minutes.** Recruiters get the facts instantly. Design leads get depth when they choose to go deeper.
5. **Restraint is the flex.** One accent, one type family, one grid. Ambition shows in precision, not quantity.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA: text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (≥ 3:1 for large text and UI), visible focus, full keyboard access, semantic structure.
- Every animation has a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative. The custom cursor, smooth scroll and the dot-field animation turn off for reduced motion and for touch.
- Content is never gated on animation. Reveals enhance text that is already there.
